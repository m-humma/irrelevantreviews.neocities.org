Synopsis

Endacopia is an indie adventure game developed by Andyl4nd, an independent Puerto Rican animator and game developer mostly known for his surrealist, strange, and unique art direction that he shows off in his original animations hosted on YouTube. The game takes place around a young boy named Mellow waking up in an unfamiliar area and having to navigate the weird worlds he encounters on his journey back home. Similarly to Andres' former works, the game encompasses the same off-putting and absurdist art direction that he utilises in his other works. I had been watching his works before the release of the game, so I was very excited to play it for myself! His art style works perfectly in a video game like this.

"Endacopia is a non-traditional Point-and-Click adventure game for Windows and Macintosh. You will be required to guide a lost child through a world that operates on foreign rules. The child’s name is “Mellow” and he doesn’t know much about anything other than the fact that he has a strong gut instinct. With a little help from his guardian angel (You!), Mellow will be able to overcome many obstacles in the way of him finding a place he can call "home"... wherever that may be."

—Andyl4nd, on the demo's itch.io page

Art

Endacopia has a very unique art style. As mentioned before, the game has a lot of features borrorwed over from Andres' former works, so it takes on a very absurdist-like art direction. The game is also very clearly inspired by the aesthetics of old 90's computer games, particularly those of the Point-and-Click genre. The game intentionally mimics this style with things like pixelated graphics, a low internal resolution, and occasional scuffy 3D animations. I think that the game pulls it off amazingly, and the aesthetic never feels too forced. It felt very natural and fit the vibe of the game. The character designs are also incredibly unique and memorable, too.


Gameplay

Endacopia features a very fun and robust combat system. You can hold right click to block, left click to punch, and you have to avoid the incoming enemy attacks as well as look for vulnerable points in their animation to punch them. I'm a super big fan of this mechanic, I found it very fun and the harder boss fights are left optional if you don't want to go through the effort of beating them. Endacopia also has a great sense of humour. The game takes advantage of the fact that the main character is just some random ass kid, and Mellow's mundane reactions to the world around him is extremely amusing, to say the least.

Endacopia features a lot of puzzles, and although I did find them a bit fun, they felt a little too hard to figure out at times. There's a character you can summon who will give you clues on what to do, but they are kinda useless most of the time. Like, if you need to find a key, the game will tell you, "Hey, Mellow, I wonder what that guy meant by needing the door to unlock...?"

This game has very linear progression. Despite that, the game has multiple endings that can occur depending on the choices you make throughout your playthrough. The game stays relatively the same until the very end, though. I do wish that the endings had much more varying routes during the process of obtaining them, but it's fine. There's a funny easter egg if you get all the endings where you get to play a silly DDR minigame with the weird blue guy that made the game.


Music

Endacopia sports a very wacky and abnormal soundtrack. The music can range from chilled, relaxed background tracks to intense and frankly sort of aggressive boss music. There's even a few lyrical songs with creepy, robotic voices. The game's soundtrack is unique in a way I've seen no other game be like. Verdict

Endacopia is a wacky, fun, and short-n-sweeet adventure game with a few dark-themed detours. It feels like a love letter to early edutainment games of the 90's and in extension the PC games of that era as a whole! I'd say it's a solid 8.5/10.
