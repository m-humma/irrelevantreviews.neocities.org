Synopsis

Parappa the Rapper is widely considered to be one of the first ever "proper" rhythm games to be made, quite literally debuting a genre. The Parappa the Rapper series... is weird. Like, reallyyyy weird. But I guess that's what gives it its charm! Parapa the Rapper was designed and directed by Masaya Matsuura, and the art and character designs by Rodney A. Greenblat. The game is very heavily inspired by western rap and hip hop culture, having the songs being fully made in English despite being a Japanese game. In the game, you follow the story of Parappa, a young pup trying to impress his crush Sunny Funny. I did not make those names up. This series is definitely a favourite of mine, but I can't deny that this game in particular has an ignorable amount of jank. This game does NOT hold up by today's standards, even in the remasters. The PSP remaster of the game, alongside the PS4 version, have a free to download Bonus Track DLC.


Art

For a PS1 game, the game's art style is beautiful. Most PS1 games at the time used traditional 3D models, which were cool and all, but were incredibly blocky and almost cheap looking if not utilisied correctly. Parappa does none of this. The game uses this paper-like artstyle, incorporating 2D characters in a 3D enviroment, which gives the Parappa the Rapper series its charm. Because of this, the style was less straining processing-wise and could look better without sacrificing any important gameplay elements. The low file size also gave way for the many cutscenes the game have, which are very charming. The decision for a paper cut-out like art style was made to fit Greenblat's character designs. Art director Rodney Greenblat comments on this further in an interview:

"That's why he [Matsuura] wanted to call it PaRappa the Rapper; he had the rap idea and the cut-out thing in his head already. All they needed from me was to draw the thing, so that's what I did."

—Rodney A. Greenblat, on Rodney Greenblat, The Mother Of Sony's Almost Mario

Gameplay

Main premise

The gameplay in Parappa is fun. Every level has a cutscene, usually of Parappa attempting (and failing) to impress his crush Sunny Funny. During the cutscene, he finds a mentor to learn a new skill (for some reason always by rapping) so he can impress Sunny with it. It's a fun premise, and I usually never skip the cutscenes if I'm replaying the whole game. In the levels, there is a bar on top of the screen. A marker will fly over an arrangmnt of icons of different buttons on your PlayStation controller, and you need to press said buttons in time of when they appear. Pressing these buttons in order will make Parappa say phrases, usually just repeating whatever his mentor says. This is a really fun and innovative gameplay style, but it does NOT hold up today. The timings are wonky and sometimes frustrating, and usually takes a good half-hour to even remotely learn how to time your inputs. It is very fun when you learn how to play, but it is incredibly hard for newer and especially younger players. It doesn't help that the game's "easy" mode stops after Level 3 and forces you to play normally through all other levels to progress. Again, very innovative and fun, but its held back by the technology of the time. Another issue is that the game is quite short. It's only 7 levels long, and takes around 2-3 hours to beat depending on your skillset. But it's not too much of an issue.


Freestyling and COOL mode

Freestyling is so ridiculously hard to figure out that most casual players have never even experienced it. Freestyling is when you make Parappa play DIFFERENT than whats told on screen while still managing to be on beat, and if you do well enough, your mentor runs off screen and lets you rap all by yourself. This is a cool, fun, and unique idea, but it is executed so poorly. The game's idea of "rhythm" is mindless button mashing and throwing whatever works at the wall. It's impossible to do without coming up with some insanely stupid strategy for freestyling. You can easily get past the entirety of Level 3 by getting into COOL mode and spamming triangle the entire stage. It's full of missed potential.


Music

The Parappa the Rapper series is known for it's amazing soundtracks. The songs in game are catchy and fun, and get in my head way too often. The music also changes depending on how well you're doing in-game, adding more honks and squeeks the worse you do. It's a nice touch, and really adds to the experience for some of these levels.

DLC

Bonus Track DLC

This DLC is pretty lackluster. There's simply just variations of levels where the music is swapped to a different genre, but that's it. They don't even have a different chart. It's not bad, just kinda empty...


Verdict

Parapppa the Rapper is a revolutionary game, it being charming and oozing with life, but is unfortunately held back by the technology of its time. For that, this game gets a fine but not bad 6.5/10.
