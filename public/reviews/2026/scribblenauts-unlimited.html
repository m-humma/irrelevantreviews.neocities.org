Synopsis

Scribblenauts Unlimited is the fourth installment of the Scribblenauts series. It was developed by 5th Cell and published by Warner Bros. Games. The game acts like the ultimate entry for series, having the most adjectives and objects in the entire series. Since then, the game has been ported to several newer platforms including Steam, which has added custom objects using the Steam Workshop. The game was also one of the launch titles for the Wii U.


Art

The art in Scribblenauts Unlimited is incredibly pleasing to look at. The game takes on this homey, cutesy art style that feels really fun to look at. All of the hundreds of possible objects and effects are illustrated simply, very simply, yet it feels incredibly nice to look at. I mean, look at it! It's adorable! The opening and closing cutscenes are also animated with a surprising amount of quality.

"We re-drew all the backgrounds for Scribblenauts Unlimited to have that HD feel. The characters and objects were updates from the iOS version. [...] The Wii U was a great opportunity to work on a launch title"

—Jeremiah Slaczka, on r/IAmA
"The hardware on the Wii U is far beyond what the DS provided. We’re able to have high resolution, hand-drawn environments. All of our object art is now vector art, so we don’t lose visual quality when we scale objects. We’ve done away with the budge thermometer from previous games and let players go wild and add way more objects to the level than they ever could."

—Brittany Aubert, on Scribblenauts Unlimited Wii U Interview with 5th Cell

Gameplay

Premise

The premise of Scribblenauts Unlimited is basic but also novel. You play as a young boy named Maxwell who owns a magical notebook that can summon, well, quite literally anything. I mean, it's not quite literally everything, it is just a game after all, but it is damn near impossible to find a common object that hasn't been programmed into this game. In the game, you have to help other people by solving their probles with this magic notebook. This, obviously, must've taken an immense amount of effort to implement. It's still impressive today how a Wii U game has so much content jam-packed into it!


Puzzles

Every puzzle in this game probably have at least 12 different solutions. That's just how non-linear the puzzle design in this game is! The puzzles are funny and often times the solutions turn out quite comedic. The game is incredibly non-linear, levels being unlcoked in batches so you're free to do whatever you want. There are two types of problems you can solve: Starites and Starite Shards. Starite Shards are the run-of-the-mill problems, you have one problem to solve and that's it. Simple, but effective. And also slightly addictive. Starites are a compliation of related problems bundled together and put into a type of mini-level. These are really fun and are usually also really humourous. The only complaint I have is that the the puzzles can feel a bit tedious and stale near the end of the game.


Music

The music in Scribblenauts is cheerful and fun. The music is very good at setting the atmosphere of each individual level and makes each stage feel unique! The jingles also are very, very fun.

Verdict

Scribblenauts Unlimited is a wonderful, well-made and unique game that still feels fresh even after more than a decade later! For that reason, I'm giving this game a solid 9/10.
