Synopsis

Gitaroo Man is a 2001 rhythm game developed by iNIS and published by Koei. The game follows a young boy named U-1 (get it, "you won"?) and his talking dog Puma as they fight against multiple enemies sent down by tyrannical Prince Zowie, leader of the Gravilian Empire. Then U-1 has to collect the seven magical gitaroo to become the ultimate Gitaroo Man, or something. Look, the story goes by so fast it's hard to remember anything! Anyways, in the game, you fight off different enemies using your legendary Gitaroo. The game features multiple different music artists such as COIL, Tomohiro Harada, and YUAN.


Art

Giataroo Man's art style is very pleasing to look at. It features this hand-drawn esque and oddly punk art style that's hard to find anywhere else. The unique art style compliments the gameplay and music really well. The game is mainly in 3D, alongside roughly drawn 2D UI elements. The game uses multiple mediums to get its point across and it does so wonderfully!


Gameplay

Gitaroo Man has multiple phases: Charge, Attack, and Guard. In Charge, there is a dot at the centre of the screen. From there, you spin your analogue stick to follow traced lines that come crashing into your dot. On these lines, there are red bumpers. When these reach your dot, you have to hold a button while still moving your analogue stick to the traced line. Successful notes restore your health. In Attack, the same thing follows, except successful notes attack the opponent. It's hard to explain but it's easier if you look at a picture. In Defend, you have to press the different PlayStation buttons on your controller depending on which button graphic crashes into your dot. The prior trace lines may also appear. Unsuccessful notes damage you. This gameplay dynamic is incredibly unique and fun to play, though timing notes can feel quite unresponsive at times, which has caused a lot of frustration. There is also a Master's Play option once you beat the game for the first time. In this mode, the charts are harder and mistakes hurt more. It's fun, but it isn't really my thing. There is also a versus mode you can play with freinds. There are cutscenes in-between levels, which are fun to watch, but they happen so quickly it's hard to keep track and get any real value out of the story.


Music

The music selection in Gitaroo Man is very diverse, and sounds great as well. The songs were made by a few afforementioned artists such as COIL, Tomohiro Harada, and YUAN. Each stage usually encompasses it's own genre, and has a very wide scope when it comes to music selection. One level can be rock 'n' roll, while the next one could be a salsa! Even with the stark difference between the direction chosen for each track, it never feels too jarring while also sounding incredibly catchy and fun. My personal favourite is Flyin' To Your Heart, the track for Stage 2.

Verdict

Gitaroo Man is a fun yet flawed game that could honestly REALLY use a proper remake or sequel for modern systems. But, at the time as I write this, this game will remain at an okay 6/10.
