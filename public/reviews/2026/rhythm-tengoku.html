Synopsis

Rhythm Tengoku is the debut game for the Rhythm Heaven series, which is widely considered to be one of the most important games in the Rhythm genre, deservingly so. Rhythm Tengoku has you play through 48 differing rhythm themed minigames that require you to play by tapping your inputs to the rhythm of the song that specific game has been given. The game was developed in partnership with popular Japanese singer ♂Tsunku, and the art direction done by Ko Tackeuchi, who you may recognise from the WarioWare series. This is for good reason, since both series are developed by the same company, Nintendo SPD (now merged with Nintendo). The game was exclusively released in Japan, and had no english release or offical translation. But enough blabbering, you probably only want to know if these minigames hold up today!


Gameplay

Main campaign and mechanics

The main premise is simple. You get to play through a bunch of short rhythm-based minigames throughout 8 sets of 48 games. These rhythm games, to put it lightly, are really, really fun! Every rhythm game has you put into a fun little scenario where you have to act out inputs to the rhythm of the music (e.g., releasing fireworks into the sky when the voice says "Go!"). Something that is extremely well known about the Rhythm Heaven series is that most of the minigames can be played with your eyes closed. This is because all of your inputs have audible cues right behind them, and you need to listen to them in order to be remotely good at the game. Not only is this a very refreshing premise, since most rhythm games at the time (and arguably even now) followed the same trope of pressing buttons to a charted song with notes falling down on time to the music. Rhythm Tengoku was intentionally different for its time, as expressed by the series creator:

"The [arcade] games are fun, of course — learning the sequence of on-screen prompts and hitting them in time is enjoyable. But as a musician, I sometimes thought, "Hmm, why is the marker showing up here?" It's not wrong, since it's a game after all, but as the producer of that song, I felt like, "The accent should be here..." "

—Tsunku♂, on Learn Rhythm Theory from Tsunku♂: UP your flow with Rhythm Tengoku!

One of the main mechanics in Rhythm Tengoku are Remixes. Every set of minigames in Rhythm Tengoku has 5 minigames and one remix. This remix will encompass all 5 minigames from that set and cut them up into segments, and then play them as one single level to an orignal song. Just like the minigames, these remixes are are really, really fun, even more so than the minigames. They help keep things fresh and make great advantage of all the minigames in a set. One of my favourte things about the Rhythm Heaven series are the final remixes, which are the final levels of the game and use every single minigame in the game. For a first try, Rhythm Tengoku executes this beautifully. Some remixes encompass custom graphics or even have lyrics in their songs, which is just the cherry on top for a lot of these levels. Something I really like is the perfect campaign. Occasionally players will be challenged to beat an already completed level, and I feel it's just such a creative idea that gives players incentive to replay old levels! Though, it can be really hard to perfect some of them.

That being said, I still have a few problems with this game. I don't like the sequels. Sequels are copies of previous minigames but made harder and given special graphics. I don't like these levels since they feel like a really lazy addition to the game as they don't offer much and hog up more than a third of the total playtime. They feel boring and stale. I understand that there is a limited amount of device storage and limited development time, but they feel like a hassle to complete and I think the game would be better having no sequels and ending at Remix 6 than having any sequels at all. Another issue is that the game has a really steep learning curve. It can feel frustrating for new and especially young players at first, and it takes a bit to get used to the gameplay and overall jank of some of the games. Later installments fix this issue.


Side modes

The side modes in this game are pretty lackluster. The Rhythm Toys are entertaining for only a minute, most players don't even know that Live exists, and the Drum Lessons are so tedious they bore you out before you can even get two thirds through the level. The only good side modes are the Endless Games. These are actually pretty entertaining and fun... except Quiz EX. We don't talk about Quiz EX. The credits are also fun to watch, but that's pretty common for most Nintendo games.


Music

Obviously a rhythm game is going to have good music, but WOW this game has great music. Every stage has amazing music, but the remixes especially. The ones with lyrics are arguably the best ones. For a GBA game, the songs are surprisingly high effort, and even with the compression the studio made ones sound amazing. Rhythm Tengoku in my opinion has the weakest sountrack in the series, but the songs still hold up fairly well today. My personal favourite is WISH Kimi o Matenakute, the stage for Remix 5.

Verdict

Rhythm Tengoku is a wonderful little game that was a start of a great series, but is a bit janky in its current state and has a few major flaws holding it back. For that, this game gets a cool 7.5/10.
