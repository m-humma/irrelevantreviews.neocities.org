Synopsis

Paper Mario: Color Splash is the fifth installment of the Paper Mario series and acts a spiritual successor to the series' prior game, Paper Mario: Sticker Star. The game is generally seen negatively, due to its uninnovative gameplay and repititive battle system, though much of that negativity is also unfairly clipped alongside the stigma of its relation to Sticker Star, which is widely regarded as the worst in the series! Despite its flaws, it is still rarely praised for its witty and funny dialogue. Quite the mixed bag of a game, huh?


Art

The art direction in frankly all of Paper Mario is amazing, but the direction that Color Splash takes is just executed superbly. Color Splash is the first HD Paper Mario game in the series, after all. The game utilises realistic crafts items such as construction paper and cardboard in a 3D enviroment with 2D characters made out of paper. It just looks beautiful! The game feels like an actual arts and crafts project at times! It is literally impossible to find a place that doesn't look pleasing to look at. The art style is probably one of the best parts of the game. Just look at that CARDBOARD!


Gameplay

Story

Whilst on a journey at sea, Princess Peach and Mario recieve a letter stamped from a place named Port Prisma. Once Mario opens the letter, he discovers it wasn't a letter at all, and it was instead a Toad with all of its color drained! (So basically a dead corpse...) Obviously intrigued, they both sail off to Port Prisma to find the source of this. It's then revealed that Bowser had sent out his army to steal all of the paint in Port Prisma by sucking the color out of everything! That isn't a metaphor, the Shy Guys actually suck the paint out with straws. Eventually, Mario and Peach meet an alive paint can named Huey who is the guardian of the Big Paint Stars. That were stolen by Bowser. Oops? Anyway, you wake him up, he tells you that we need to get the Paint Stars back, blah blah blah Peach gets kidnapped you get the gist, you get a cool hammer, and now you have to run around collecting the Paint Stars. This sounds like an intriguing story at first, but it is just so uninteresting! It's a Mario game, I'm not asking for some shakespearean-level tale, but Paper Mario is known for its nuanced and incredibly interesting narratives. And then for whatever reason they lock in with the ending and (vague spoilers) make a huge, impactful sacrifice?? If I could plot the story's quality on a graph, it'd be high on the start, high on the end, and straight to the bottom for everything else. And that weirdly feels more insulting than having the story go downhill the entire game.


Dialogue

The dialogue, on the other hand, is the absolute polar opposite. The Paper Mario series is already known for its witty and funny dialogue, but Paper Mario: Color Splash just takes it to the next level. The game's dialogue is just so uncannily funny its impossible to play a session of this game without laughing. It's this perfect mix of self-conscious irony and witty wordplay that just makes the dialogue in the game so funny to play through. I don't think there's a single joke that doesn't land! For such a bland story, the characters are surprisingly full of charm and personality, and all of the gags and one-off jokes are super memorable. I've laughed out loud at this game more times than I'd like to admit...


Battle system

Okay, this is when it gets really bad. To be quick, the battle system in Paper Mario: Color Splash is absolute ass. The game uses a card system where you collect cards as you play the game and you can use them in battle. The problem? There are no health bars. Not even any numbers or anything! The enemies progressively lose paint every time they're attacked, the less paint the less health, so there's technically a health indicator, but it means you just have to guess whether or not the cards you're playing will be strong enough actually kill the enemies or not. Not only that, but the amount of cards you play increase as you play the game. That usually would be be fine, but it means that for the majority of the game you have only one or two card slots. WHAT'S THE STRATEGY HERE? AM I MEANT TO JUST THROW STUFF AT THE WALL?? You're telling me I just have to USE the same card OVER AND OVER again? Do you realise how boring that is!? The battle system is so bad that I get annoyed whenever it's required to beat an enemy to progress. I literally avoid enemies so I don't have to fight them! If that isn't horrid game design, then I don't know what is!


Colorization

In the game, you can walk past colorless spots sucked out by the shy guys as described earlier. You can then use your hammer to paint these spots and restore them back to their original form. This process is known in-game as "Colorization". It sounds cool on paper (pun intended), but there is literally almost no incentive to do this. Coloring in these spots gets you cards and coins, but both of these items are already so abundant that it's almost redundant to go out of your way to paint them. It's fun at first, but it is never something you really never care about unless you're a masochist who's trying to 100% the game.


Miscellaneous features

Roshambo Temples are optional levels in the game that have three rounds of Roshambo (this game's version of Rock Paper Scissors). The first two rounds are pre-determined, and the two toads guarding the temple give you hidden hints in their dialogue pointing to what you should play. The third round is entirely random. What half-eaten donut at Nintendo thought that an entirely RNG-based Rock Paper Scissors game would be fun? It's just luck! Literally just luck!

And very rarely, everytime you return to the world map after beating a level, the "Shy Bandit" will spawn and move to a random level on the map to suck the color out. Reaching him in time will start a battle, but not reaching him before the time runs out will result in the level the Shy Bandit is on to LOSE all of its Colorization progress. How is this FUN? It's baffling to me that anyone thought this was a good idea! And if you're particularly unlucky, there's an incredibly rare chance that the game will screw you over and spawn the Shy Bandit SO far away it's physically impossible to reach him! How did this get into the game??


Music

One of best things in this game is the music. It's catchy, upbeat, and genuinely just super fun to listen to. They're the only thing that makes the battle sequences bareable. What else can I say? Just listen to it for yourself!

Verdict

Paper Mario: Color Splash is one of the best examples of how detrimental bad game design can be for a game. Quite literally everything about this game is amazing except the gameplay, and look how it's come out! It could've been so great, but unfortunately it's going to have to stay as a 4.5/10.
