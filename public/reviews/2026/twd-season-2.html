Synopsis

The Walking Dead: Season Two is the second mainline entry in Telltale's The Walking Dead series, based off of the comic book series of the same name. Taking place after the events of Season One, the game's narrative focuses on Clementine, a reoccuring character from the last game who the player now plays as throughout the game. The game features five 2-hourish long episodes, which were released throughout very late 2013 to mid 2014. Choices from the first game can be optionally carried over to impact minor parts of the story in the second game such as dialogue and references to past events.


Art

There isn't really much to say that I already haven't said. The game's art direction is basically identical to the its predecessor, and it still looks great! It's a very nice stylised realistic style that matches well with the general atmosphere and feel of the game. It looks gritty and rough, but it's still soft enough for the game to not feel entirely depressing.


Gameplay

UI changes

This is one of the only aspects in the game where it feels more like a downgrade than an upgrade. Comparitively to the prior entry, the UI for carrying out actions feels very clunky. As opposed to a scroll wheel, the game opts for a rotary-like dial. In my opinion, the scrollwheel used in the prequel was much easier to use. Also, there's a sidebar with all the items you have -- fine, I should know what's in my inventory -- but you can switch between items even though it does... literally nothing. Visually or functionally. The only UI that got an upgrade is probably the dialogue choice options.


Story

If I were to be honest, I would say that Season Two has a superior handling of the story. It feels way more impactful and long-lasting than the narrative in Season One, which is an insane feat, considering that Season One already has an incredibly well-crafted story! The events that occur feel real, they feel immersive and most importantly don't feel scared to show off the disgusting acts caused by the urgency of an apocolapse. Almost every aspect of the game's immaculate storytelling has been improved upon from the last game. I can't think of a single major flaw I have with the writing. Every character feels like a real person, and I never felt as if the story was showing any major immersion-breaking exposition.


Depictions of Abuse

Okay, this part is going to be a bit dicey, so bare with me. This part is going to include a lot of disturbing content, so the most sensitive parts are spoiler tagged. I don't like sugarcoating things, so I'm going to be very blunt here.

The third episode of the Walking Dead's second season, In Harm's Way, has an exceptionally well-made depiction of physical abuse. I absolutely hated playing through this episode. It made my blood boil, skin crawl, and my conscience uncomfortable. Which is what makes this episode so great. Abuse is meant to feel uncomfortable. It isn't something you should be able to just shake off, and the characters demonstrate that superbly. It feels too real, like you're watching something you really shouldn't be seeing. Multiple times in the episode, characters are hit and slapped, including the children. Sarah and Clementine are both hit at least once. And it just feels awful, every time. The game does an amazing job at making you feel off without constantly shoving gore or blood in your face. Of course, there is blood, but most the time the actual unnervingness comes from the pain inflicted opposed to the visuals, if that makes sense.

One of the characters, a pregnant lady known as Rebecca, is implied to have been raped, or at least sexually assaulted in some way. Near the middle of the episode, she tells Clementine that the baby is not her husband's, but she never states how, so the player is inclined to believe she had cheated. She even said that her husband would “kill her” if he found out, further emphasising this. Looking back this was most likely her blaming herself for the incident, which is unfortunately common in many people who have experienced similar traumas. Later in the episode, the rapist, named Carver, says to Rebecca: “The fuck are you looking at, bitch? Don't act like you didn't love every second of it--” before getting his head smashed in by a crowbar (deserved). Even before this Carver had been constantly catcalling and speaking to her with an eerie faux-sweet tone of voice throughout the episode, which leads the player to slowly realise over time that Rebecca had a worse relationship with Carver than once thought. This is an incredible display of quality storytelling. The game never spoon-feeds you, it lets you form your own interpretation until the actual truth clicks and hits you like a truck. It wouldn't be as nearly as emotionally impactful if they had simply given her unrealistic dialogue shouting “Hey, 11-year-old kid, here's all the trauma I have experienced up to this point conveniently listed out in a way the player would understand.”


Music

The music in The Walking Dead: Season Two greatly serves and fits the general atmosphere of the game. What else do you want me to say? Also, remember the credits song for Season One having lyrics? Well, now every credits song is like that! Neat!
