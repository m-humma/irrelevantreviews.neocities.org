Synopsis

Katamari Damacy is the first installment in the Katamari game series. It is incredibly highly acclaimed, being known for its wacky, colourful, and oddly charming art style and unique gameplay style. The game follows a young prince being commanded by an all-powerful space deity king to rebuild all the stars in Space after the King had accidentally destroyed all that was in the cosmos. The young prince does this by rolling a magical ball known as the Katamari -- an adhesive sphere that picks up anything smaller than itself and grows every item stuck upon it. Did you get ANY of that? If not, too bad! I'm not re-explaining that fever dream of a plot. The game's director, Keita Takahashi, obviously strugled to pitch his idea to Namco, which is ironic considering the game's surprising success in both the Japanese and North American releases.


Art

Katamari Damacy's art style is weird, wacky, colourful, and at times even psychedelic. The bold visuals and unique art direction not only helped Katamari Damacy stand out from other PS2 titles, but also the whole Katamari game series as a whole. The in-game models were kept very simple as a lot of objects needed to be rendered on screen at once, which is why most of the game sports a charming low-poly art style. Katamari Damacy's art style is captivating and charming.

"My understanding was the GPU was not so strong in the PS2, but we want to show a lot of objects as much as possible. So each single object would need to be very simple, low poly. and then, if they’re low poly then I know this is some decision for the art style, but for me, if it's low poly, then the texture shouldn't be very realistic. The texture also should be simple. Easy to understand. [...] I know many people love that simpleness and clean texture, like, what… Minecraft."

—Keita Takahashi, on Interview Transcript: Keita Takahashi, May 2025

Gameplay

Controls

The control scheme is simply genius. Point both control sticks up or down to move forward or backward, and point each control stick to the opposite of each other to turn. You can dash by rapidly alternating both controls sticks up and down. Creating a full movement system with only the control sticks is absolutely insane! Admittedly, the controls can feel a little wonky at first. It takes a few levels to get used to. But once you do understand it, it feels awesome to play. Dashing is hard to pull off but feels insanely rewarding when done right. With how much fun I've had with this game, I wouldn't be surpised if my controller's joysticks hate me now...


Make A Star

In Katamari Damacy, there are two level types. The first type is the "Make A Star" level. In these levels, you simply roll up a landscape up into a ball using the afforementioned Katamari and release that amalgamation into the night sky to be turned into a star. You need to get your katamari ball as big as the pre-set goal each level has. If you want, you can end the level early, but you're heavily encouraged to keep playing after you hit that goal to make the biggest star you can! This is a super fun gameplay loop, and has a lot of replayability. The beginning is a bit of a slow burn, but it gets more and more fun the bigger your ball gets. The later parts where you're big enough to pick up people and even buildings are also super fun. After you've released your star, you can view that star sit in the galaxy in a different menu. It's a great addition that has had me entranced a surprising amount of times. The level layouts are also masterfully designed. The levels are easy to follow without feeling too linear. It's also a miracle how they got such a power-demanding game to run on the PS2. The last level is my favourite. Minor spoilers: In the final level, you create the Moon. This level is especially fun as you become so big to the point you can roll over buildings, islands, and even clouds!


Constellations

Constellations are the second type of levels in Katamari Damacy. When completed, they represent real-life constellations in the afforementioned galaxy you can view. These constellations are the ones used in real-life astrology. As opposed to the Make A Star levels which ask for the biggest ball possible, constellation levels have a certain unique gimmick to them, usually themed around their astrological roots. For example, the level for Cancer, my personal favourite, requires you to collect as many crabs as possible, completely ignoring the size of your katamari in the scoring. These levels are particularly fun, even more than the regular ones.


Collections

There is one main collectible: Objects. Objects are the different things that you roll up into your ball. Every time you roll a unique object, it is logged and added to a collection. This ranges from animals, food, buildings, toys, signs, furniture, vehicles, and even people! There's a whopping 1438 objects in the game, which is insane! Seriously, how did they even fit all of this in a PS2 game...? They're obsessively fun to collect.


Music

The music in this game is immaculate. The music tracks that play during the game are upbeat, fun, and amazing to listen to. All of the songs are unique and enhance the game in a way I've never see any other game do. My personal favourites are Lonely Rolling Star and the credits theme. I am also a big fan of the song that plays when you're viewing your constellations. It does a perfect job of making you feel small compared to the endless void that is the night sky.

Verdict

Katamari Damacy is a masterfully crafted game with a unique and fun premise. I'll definitely still be playing it even when I finish this review! For that reason, this game gets a 10/10 for me.
