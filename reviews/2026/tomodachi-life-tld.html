Synopsis

Tomodachi Life: Living the Dream is the 2026 successor to its simply titled predecessor Tomodachi Life on 3DS. The game is a simulation game -- using the Switch's Mii avatar system to represent people. You tend to an island chock full of these Miis, similar to its afforementioned predecessor, only difference being that the original had you tend to Miis in an apartment building. The game features much more complex customisation options, as well as deeper mechanics and more nuanced gameplay that compared to the prior entry. I was quite fond of the original Tomodachi Life, so I was excited to hear that it was going to get a sequel for a much more modern console! Although I do have a few gripes with this game, I still thoroughly enjoyed it... kind of.


Art

Living the Dream's art style is unironically gorgeous. Before this game, most Mii-centred games would simply build off the pre-existing art style of the Miis. This wasn't bad by any means, but it felt a bit generic after a while. Living the Dream, however, completely breaks this rule. The game uses an effortlessly clean look, using colourful models and lineless shapes to convey itself. The world is cartoony and fun while the UI bubbly and clean. It look as fun as it actually is. Some of the scenery looks almost beautiful in certain cutscenes, especially the beach areas.


Gameplay

Mii Customisation

Compared to the previous entry, the customisation is now much more vast. Compared to the base Mii creator in the Switch settings menu, the Mii creator in Living the Dream has a much bigger variety of options when creating your Miis. Theres more hair styles, skin colours, mouth shapes, eye shapes, noses, accessories, and much, much more. It's impossible to state just how much of an upgrade this Mii creator is compared to the one included with the console. I'll admit, making Miis is pretty fun with this editor. Another feature is facepaint. You can draw on your Mii to add features that you couldn't do with the preset options. This would be great... if it wasn't for one underlying flaw. You can only draw over or under the Mii's actual features, meaning if you draw over their eyes, they just kinda stare at you and never blink. In Miitopia, a much older Mii-centred game also ported to Switch, you can choose which feature you're drawing on, meaning if you draw on the eye layer your custom eye will be treated appropriately and hide whenever the Mii has to blink. Living the Dream does not have feature, which is immensively disappointing. The editor is just almost perfect, which is why it's so frustrating to use at times.

The island customisation in Living the Dream is nothing short of incredible. Building is fun to do, and the progression in getting new items to build with is also great. Some time into the game, you unlock a Studio Workshop in which you can use to create custom foods, treasures, objects, and clothing. The customisation is something I do really enjoy, but at times it feels like the vast customisation is just a lazy excuse for the player to just create the gameplay for themself, for a reason I will get to in a second.


Progression

During the game, you unlock warm fuzzies whenever you make a Mii happy. If you get enough warm fuzzies, your Island Rank goes up by one and you unlock a new quirk, interior, object, or even more. This progression is simple but effective, though after Rank 40 picking out an item to unlock gets really annoying. Not only that racking up warm fuzzies can be really tedious unless you play for hours at a time, which very much leads to burnout.


Miis and Interactions

One of my favourite things about Living the Dream are the little quirks. Little quirks are quirks that you can give to your Miis when they level up. It really helps flesh out a Mii and makes them feel a lot more like the person they're meant to represent.

"[...] If we forced those quirks into the 16 personality types, you’d end up with a Mii that doesn’t feel like the person you intended to create. So we thought if players could add in those details themselves, they could have fun creating Mii characters that feel even closer to the people they're based on."

—Ryotaro Takahashi, on Ask the Developers - Volume 21

One of my main problems with Living the Dream when compared to its predecessor is an underlying lack of content. After the 20-ish hour mark, you've most likely already seen every Mii interaction, relationship status, dream, and a lot of the other features. In the prior entry, Tomodachi Life, it felt like you would never run out of scenes or interactions to see. The complete disregard for meaningful content causes the game to get really stale and boring after a few weeks of playing. This is what I meant by the customisation feeling like a lazy excuse to limit gameplay, as it feels like the game expects you to do all the work. Not only that, the game has definitely lost its "weird" factor. It feels a lot more tame compared to the prequel on 3DS. Of course it's still weird at times, just not to the extent of that of previous entries. Living the Dream by itself is an amazing game, but as a Tomodachi Life sequel? It falls flat.


Music

The music in all the Tomodachi Life games always add a huge amount atmosphere, and Living the Dream isn't an exception to that. The music in the game is composed amazingly as it manages to set the mood before you even know whats going on while still managing to be catchy! The music can range from being upbeat and happy, to melancholic and sad, to aggresive and angry, and even more. The game wouldn't be the same without its soundtrack.

Verdict

Living the Dream is so close to being great, but has such underlying flaws that somehow got overlooked during development. The game focused so much on customisation that the actual content feels unfinished. For that reason, I'm giving this game a 7/10.
