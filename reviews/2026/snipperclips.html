Synopsis

Snipperclips is one of the launch titles for the Nintendo Switch and it is probably the most underrated Nintendo game I've ever played. The game revolves around the gimmick of characters being able to snip each other into any shape they'd like, which set up the foundation of how puzzles are laid out. Not only is this an incredibly unique and fresh premise, it is executed amazingly! The puzzles in Snipperclips are challenging yet never feel too taxing, and the progression lets you play in such a way it lets you go at your own pace. The DLC, Snipperclips Plus, expands on the game perfectly and still manages to feel fresh even without bending the rules of prior base game. Surpisingly, the game is not directly made by Nintendo. It is instead developed by indie studio SFB Games, with Nintendo only marketing and publishing the game.


Art

Snipperclips is simply gorgeous to look at. The style replicates a hand-drawn aesthetic that feels like something you'd make in an arts and crafts class. It is made masterfully and never gets old to look at. The assets used in levels are cartoony and wacky, and the different in-game worlds keep aesthetics fresh by changing up the theme. Snipperclips' artstyle is endearing in a way that I've yet to find a game with such a wacky atmosphere quite like it!


Gameplay

Controls and Main Campaign

The controls feel incredibly fluid and responsive and any mistakes you make never feel as if they're your fault. The actual puzzles in-game are charming and fun. Most of them are simple, e.g. one of the most common type of level is cutting your characters into a uniquely shaped sillouette. The more complex levels, however, are head-scratching and a bit difficult to figure out without sacraficing the actual fun aspect of puzzles, which is something other puzzle games tend to forget! Another thing is progression, which is handled amazingly well. You actually don't have to beat all levels to beat the game. The only thing you need to do is beat a set amount of levels and the final level, which means you can leave any levels you might be stuck on to come back later. This is extremely convenient, but don't worry, I've played all the levels, so I haven't missed any content to write this review. I think.


Puzzles

The puzzles in Snipperclips are insanely well-deisgned. Every level feels fun and builds off of its descendants without relying too on much certain mechanics. Every puzzle really scratches both you and your second player brains, and never feels unfair or overly challenging. A problem I have with a lot of puzzle games is that they are often times too clever, which usually results in me looking up a guide and ruining the fun of the puzzle. Snipperclips does NOT have this problem. Every puzzle is given a clear objective, and it's up to you and a friend to figure it out. Puzzles are rewarding, and don't feel like a chore.


Extra Modes

The main campaign, World Mode is the 1-2 player mode, which has 3 worlds. It might not seem like a lot, but the extra modes more than makes for it. Alongside, there's a 2-4 player mode, Part Mode, which is just as good as the main campaign, and just as long. I haven't played it nearly as much as the main campaign -- mainly because I don't know enough people who aren't nearly as unemployed as me -- but the times I have played it was a blast, arguable even more fun than the main campaign. My personal favourite is Blitz Mode, which has no puzzles, but instead minigames. There's a basketball one, a hokcey one, and my personal favourite, Dojo. This one is my faourite because you get to kill people in it. Well, er, it's more like snip -- but my point still stands! In my opinion, Blitz Mode is the best part in the game. My only complaint is that 2 out the 3 minigames in Blitx Mode don't work in 3-player mode, despite being labelled as "2-4 players". Well, it technically works, but the third player kind of just stands there while the other two have fun. Being honest, there isn't much you can do to make a 3-player basketball, but there could at least a different layout for if there's 3 players in hockey! It's very minor, but it still kind of peeves me.


Music

The music in Snipperclips is extremely charming, and surprsingly catachy. It was composed by bo en, who may funnily enough recognise from the psychological horror game OMORI. The soundtrack is energetic and fun the whole way through, and it is a joy to listen to. The game encapsulates itself perfectly with the music alone!

DLC

Snipperclips Plus

Snipperclips Plus introduces two new worlds, and it's than better than the last three! Newer, more interesting mechanics are added, and the levels are fun and unique. Theres also new minigames for Blitz Mode: Territory, Keepaway, and Roundup. These are in my opinion the better half of the minigames. My personal favourite is Territory, where you have to paint the most on a blank sheet of paper. There's also a new mode: Stamps. This mode lets you paint on a blank canvas with your characters, and it's really relaxin to use. It's unironically one of the best parts of the DLC.


Verdict

Snipperclips is a fun, unique, and well-designed that is unfortunately shadowed by the giants that are the rest of the Switch's launch titles. It's an underappreciated gem and for me, it gets a great 10/10.
