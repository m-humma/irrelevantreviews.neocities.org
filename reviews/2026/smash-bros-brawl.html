Synopsis

Super Smash Bros. Brawl is the third installment of the Super Smash Bros. series, and is known for a bunch of good and bad reasons. In my opinion, Brawl is a great game -- it's super fun and detailed -- but at times it feels like a lot of this game's ambition brings it down by an unfortunate amount, which makes it seem inferior when compared to Melee (which in some aspects, it kind of is). Its story mode, Subspace Emissary, is undeniably the crown jewel of the game, by far the best story mode any Smash Bros. game has ever had. Despite it's flaws, Brawl is still an insanely solid game, and is far from being "bad", per se. The stages and atmosphere is executed wonderfully, it's just the actual gameplay is what it struggles at. That too, and a low amount of develepor time...


Art

For a Wii game, this game's artstyle is pulled off surprisingly well. Brawl's art style is meant to be much more realistic than all the other prior entries. You'd expect that to be a horrible idea, considering the Wii's god awful specs, but they somehow made it not only bareable look at but even pretty at times. It sounds ironic to say it, since almost every Triple A game uses hyper-realistic graphics nowadays, but the game's art direcion was very unique for it's time and stood out by a wide margin when compared to other titles, especially the titles on the potato that is the Wii. The game made it obvious it was intended for "casual" gamers. Whatever the hell that means.

"Of course, I think there was a possibility of taking Smash Bros. in a more casual-gaming direction. For example, we could make the art style simpler or make it possible to use Miis. However, with the various titles that the Wii was to offer, I felt that people were looking for something different."

—Masahiro Sakurai, on Iwata Asks - Volume 2


Gameplay

Group

The actual fighting in Brawl is weird compared to previous entries. The fighting is still fun, of course, but there were a lot of baffling decisions made when designing the brawling system. For one, characters feel way more floaty than they should. Something I've noticed with Brawl compared to other entries is that rounds take significantly longer than usual. The chracters are slow and floaty, and it's almost impossible to combo anyone since characters have ridiculously low stunlock. If it weren't for the bigger roster, I would see almost no reason to play this instead of just playing Melee instead. Competitively the fights were a slog too. I'm not, well, good enough at the game to know how the competitive side of any smash game works, but if you talk to any competitive player they'll tell you Brawl's fighting is broken. Brawl also introduced Final Smashes, which are fun, but it felt more like a marketing gimmick at the time.

Speaking of the roster, I think the game's choice for new characters was decent. There isn't really any character I feel like didn't deserve to get into the game. Olimar, Pit, and Wario are quite fun to play and obviously Snake and Sonic's inclusion was insane to have at the time (and even today). The only problem I have is that unlocking fighters is kind of annoying.


Solo

The content in Solo is surpisingly good. Classic Mode is super fun to play. If you're not familiar with it, Classic Mode is a mode where you get to fight against a bunch of different themed battles against CPU fighters. It's super fun, and I've played it countless times. Alongside Classic Mode, after you unlock every character, you unlock All-Star Mode. In this mode, you will fight every single fighter in the game in order of their original debut. This mode is also super fun, but it is way harder than Classic Mode. The Event Matches are great too. These make you obtain certain objectives during an array of different fights set up as missions. Target Smash and 100-Men Smash are great boredom killers when you have nothing else in the game to do. Home-Run Contest is a staple of the series, but its kinda just... there, if you know what I mean.

Subspace Emissary is the main story mode for Brawl, and being honest, it's quite overhyped. Subspace Emissary is very highly praised among a lot of people, but something about it doesn't click with me. It's not bad by any means, its quite good on its own. It just feels rushed and particularly sluggish. The actual gameplay in the levels are slow and at times even boring, with the only thing keeping you from dozing off from boredom being the combat and cutscenes. I admit that some of the levels such as the mob rush ones are quite fun, but the bad outnumbers the good by quite a bit. The cutscenes are a much better inclusion. Subspace Emissary has an overarching storyline that progresses the more you play. The different fighters all interact with each other in different ways in a fun manner. Again, these cutscenes are great, even better than the gameplay, but they feel quite stale after a while. The interactions never progress further than mindless nodding and following along and the actual proper reason for a lot of the things that happen are never explained. Most of the things that occur in the game kind of just "happens" with no proper reasoning. There's only a few moments in the game that actually made me feel something, and half of those moments were nothing more than just "hype moments" and aura. The storyline is kind of just shoved in because you can't, quote unquote, "have a game without a storyline".

"With older games, players were thrown into the game without any particular objective. With games nowadays though, you just can't do that, leading me to the conclusion that we couldn’t have a game without a storyline. With Smash Bros., however, there are way too many characters and the game is made without a main character. Therefore, I had a really hard time figuring out how we might establish a storyline."

—Masahiro Sakurai, on Iwata Asks - Volume 2

Vault

The two main collectibles in Brawl are trophies and stickers. Trophies are carried over from Melee, while stickers are a new addition. Trophies are gained from playing through the afforementioned Subspace Emissary or finishing Classic or All-Star Mode with a specific character. I'm quite fond of trophies. They're fun to collect, and shed light on lesser known game series. It's nice to read the blurbs they have. Stickers are primarily unlocked in Subspace Emissary as well. Stickers will give you perks when you fight in the story mode. These stickers are... pretty forgettable. I didn't even know they were a thing until half way through the game. They barely work and are too much of a fuss to equip to the point you never you use them. Coin Launcher is surprisingly fun, Masterpieces are fun until they expire, and Stage Builder is an interesting addition.


Music

The music in Brawl is one of the best in the series. Not only does the original music sound incredible, but the remixes are even arguably better than their original sources. Songs like Final Destination and the opening are what help make the game iconic

Verdict

Super Smash Bros. Brawl is a great game held back by its enormous ambition. It's quite the mixed bag of a game. For this reason, Brawl gets a 7/10 for me.
