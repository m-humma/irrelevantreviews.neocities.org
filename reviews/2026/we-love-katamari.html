Synopsis

We Love Katamari (stylised as We ♥︎ Katamari) is the 2005 sequel to the critically acclaimed Katamari Damacy. The game was directed by series creator Keita Takahashi, who also dircted the prior game. After the massive success of Katamari Damacy, Namco immediately ordered a sequel to be made against Takahashi's wishes. Takahashi said that he had a major disdain for sequels in the video game industry, but when told that the game's development would continue with or without his input, he begrudgingly took the role of director. The game itself is a much more refined experience compared to its predecessor, it having a much bigger budget and amount of content.


Art

The art direction in We Love Katamari is very similar to that in the prior game. In order to save resources, the game uses low-poly 3D models for its assets. Despite these limitations, the game still manages to encorprate the models into the game's overall style very well. The graphics are great fun to look at, the the visual humour featured is even funnier than before! Not only that, but the character designs have gotten much more unique and weird. The game definitely leans into a more psycheledic approach when compared to its predecessor. Katamari is just as goofy and overtly strange as it always has been.

"It may look like I'm just messing around, but really, that's what's really lacking in games today. We're making games, so come on, we need to make [them] more silly and fun... I wanted to make this game something that anybody could play casually and lightly, even more so than the original Katamari Damacy."

—Keita Takahashi, on Feature: We ♥︎ Katamari [archive]

Gameplay

Premise

Unlike the prior entry, We Love Katamari is very self-aware about the fact that it's a video game. As you play the game, the Prince and King of the Cosmos go around taking suggestions from fans -- so quite literally doing fan service. You go around rolling up things to do certain tasks, in the form of gimmick levels. The prior entry, Katamari Damacy, also featured gimmick levels, though they were much less abundant. I think that the gimmick levels being the main focus is very much a good change, as it helps the game feel considerably more fresh. Of course, some of the levels still require you to try and make the biggest Katamari you can, which is still just as fun as always. The game does a very good job at still being new while sticking to the core fundamentals. It introduces a good number of mechanics and quality-of-life changes while still feeling like a Katamari game. I ardently believe this game is a perfect sequel.

"I wanted to do the kind of stuff that we can only do in a sequel. I mean, there are many games that can't launch a sequel. A title name like We Love Katamari, a game system based on fans, or the Japanese packaging -- these all would have been impossible with a brand-new game."

—Keita Takahashi, on Feature: We ♥︎ Katamari [archive]

Levels

The levels in We Love Katamari are as effortlessly fun as always. As mentioned before, the game leans more into more gimmicky ideas when it comes to its levels, which in my opinion was a great idea. Some of my favourites are the ones where you have to pick up the most amount of a certain object. The game does everything in its power to make sure you aren't bored! My only complaint is that there's no Eternal levels, but I guess it isn't that big of a deal when the bast levels are already so good! Something that I am quite fond of is that once you complete a level, you unlock a second alternate version of that level with a slightly different goal. This is a really nice way of encourgaing replayability, and is also just dumb fun in general.


Revamped features

In the first game, Cousins were unlockable characters that could be rolled up after the first completion. They could only be used in the multiplayer mode. In comparison, Cousins in We Love Katamari are avaiable to toll up from the very start and can be used in the main game. This is a very nice change, as it makes collecting the Cousins much less of a hassle and also adds a layer of customisability to the game. The cutscenes in the sequel are much more engaging too, the cutscenes following the early life of the King of the Cosmos as opposed to the random family shown in the first game. Elements like the UI, save data, and dialogue have been refined to a tee. (Haha, get it? Because the guy who made Katamari also made a game called to a T? No? Music

Although We Love Katamari's soundtrack doesn't have as many heavy hitters when compared to Katamari Damacy, it still is absolutely phenomenal. Pieces like Katamari on the Swing and DISCO★PRINCE are what make me love this series so much.

Verdict

We Love Katamari is an almost perfect sequel to an already almost perfect game. No notes, 10/10. And just between you and me, I think I love Katamari just alright ♥︎
