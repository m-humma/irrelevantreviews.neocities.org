Synopsis

Rhythm Heaven Groove is the fifth installment of the Rhythm Heaven series, being released for the Nintendo Switch. It includes by far the most original minigames in the series, having over 80 single-player games and 30 more multiplayer games, excluding the extra side modes. The game is heavily based off of Rhythm Tengoku, the first installment of the series for the GBA, as seen with the UI design and level roster. The game is the exact opposite of a disappointment. The minigames are just as (if not more) fun as the prior games. Everything feels exciting and fresh but still keeps that old charm that the Rhythm Heaven series has always had. As a huge Rhythm Heaven fan, I can say that Groove satisfied my needs pretty well! In Japanese, the game title is "Rhythm Heaven Miracle Stars", referencing how the game's development was considered a "miracle" considering the conditions that the game producer, Tsunku♂, was at the time going thorugh. In the credits, he's even listed as a "Miracle Producer"!

[note] "During that time when I was seriously ill, I was going through a very difficult period mentally. The fact that Nintendo didn't stop developing games and continued to work towards their release became one of my beacons of hope, and it made me think, "I can do this!" "

Art

When I first played, I was concerned that I wouldn't like the game's art style as it wasn't that similar to that of the prior entries, but I'm pleasantly surprised to tell you that I actually quite enjoy this game's art direction! The game's art is cartoony and fun, with the sprites being drawn by series art director Ko Takeuchi. Something that I have also been very keen to notice is that the characters are much more diverse than compared to prior entries. There's a much more diverse set of skin tones used for characters. I also think that Takeuchi's art style has just improved in general last since he's worked on a Rhythm Heaven game.


Gameplay

Main campaign and new mechanics

Rhythm Heaven Groove's main single-player mode is great. As mentioned before, there is a surplus of minigames for you to play and they're fun and challening as always. Of course, there are a few rotten eggs that aren't exactly the best to play through, but it's like that in every game. For such a huge amount, it's impressive that so many landed so hard! The level select is also something I find quite nice to use, and the credits game is just perfect! Half-way Spoilers: The reveal for the Flipside was perfect. I had genuinely thought Remix 8 was the end of the game!

Of course, Rhythm Heaven Groove has also introduced a few new mechanics. My favourite is the feature where multiple minigames play at the same time in a way so that both minigames' cues match up, although I do complain that this feature was heavily under-utilised, only being used in a few remixes. Ending spoilers: The final column of miniames being all remixes is really cool too. It really adds up to the experience that is the final Remix. Speaking of Remix 20, I find it quite fulfilling. Of course, it isn't as good as Fever's or Megamix's, but it made me internally piss myself from adrenaline so I think it's pretty good.


Multiplayer mode and side modes

The multiplayer mode in this game is great! The games are fun and decently easy to pick up for new players. My only complaint is that the games are a bit hard for younger players, so it's really annoying to try and unlock the later levels. In my opinion, all the levels should be unlocked by default. Also, the multiplayer side modes from Fever just don't exist, for some reason.

The side modes in Groove are the best in the series. The games are addicting to play and unlock while all acting as a great incentive to go and try to earn medals in the main game. There's not a single miss!


Beatspell

Beatspell is one of the main new side modes in Groove. The player plays as a Magician, who's been accidentally summoned to a cave full of demons. You have to fight off these demons using spells. These spells can be cast by clicking out a unique rhythm that each spell has. This gameplay style is very obviously inspired by the Patapon game series, and although I wouldn't say that it's exactly like Patapon, you can defintely see the resemblance once you play both games. The game introudces many features as you go along, such as new spells, items and the ability to dodge attacks. The game is seperated into chapters that unlock the more you play the main game. The later part of each chapter can get really fun, but the first part feels incredibly slow and sluggish as the spells you unlock don't transfer through save files, and instead need to be unlocked again. The first part of each chapter is just repeating the same spell over and over again, because you just simply have nothing else to use. It definitely has potential, but the sluggishness is hard to ignore. I honestly think it would be way better if it was made into a fully fleshed-out spinoff game. At least the story is interesting...


Music

The music in Groove, like every Rhythm Heaven game, is superb! The music is catchy, outgoing, and energetic. The songs get stuck in your head more times than you think, and it's hard not to bop your head to the beat while you're playing.

Verdict

Rhythm Heaven Groove is an amazing comeback to one of my favourite series'! It manages to be new and exciting while still sticking to its series' roots. For that reason I'm giving this game a perfect 10/10.
