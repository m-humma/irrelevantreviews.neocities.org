Synopsis

LocoRoco is a fun, unique, and challenging little game from 2006. It is an absolute must-have for all PSP owners, and is critically acclaimed for its innovative gameplay, fun artstyle, and unique music. It was directed by Tsutomu Kouno, who has also worked for the fairly obscure PS2 game ICO. LocoRoco is a super unique game, in the sense no other is quite like it. It's a great game, oozing with charm, and it basks in all of its, er, "weirdness". Don't get me wrong, that's a good thing!


Art

LocoRoco's artstyle, like everything else in the game, is incredibly unique. It has this very homey feel, its art being very streamlined and smooth. The game's style is simple, yet so lovely to look at anyway. The game's art is pure eyecandy. The game definitely feels at home in the PSP, as said by the game's director, Tsutomu Kouno:

"When the PSP was first announced, it seemed to me like everyone was gearing up to make these complicated games for it, like sequels to games that had been on the PS2. I wanted to break that mold and make a game that really seemed at home on the PSP."

—Tsutomu Kouno, on The Thoughtful Design of LocoRoco: Tsutomu Kouno Speaks

Gameplay

Main story and mechanics

LocoRoco's story is a very simple one. The LocoRoco (the blobs you play as) are attacked by the Moja troop. The LocoRoco, and their freinds the Mui Mui, have to go on and defeat the Moja troop once and for all. Very juvenile... but simple enough for such a relaxed game. The gameplay is simple. Use the L and R buttons to turn the planet. Your LocoRoco will follow your lead through gravity, and you need to get through levels through these limited controls. Pressing L and R at the the same time will make them jump, which is tricky to do smoothly while moving. At first, the controls can feel frustrating, but they'll be mastered by the time you finish your first two levels. The controls are, well, simply genius! The fact a game with such a sophisticated level design philosophy encapsulates its controls into nothing but two buttons is nothing but a miracle. Every level has you start out with one LocoRoco. You can grow up to 20 by eating fruits that pass you by. You lose by losing all LocoRoco, or win by finsihing the level. You can split and merge your LocoRoco into one, bigger, fatter LocoRoco to solve puzzles. Not only is this mechanic just a fun idea, it is incredibly well executed and is one of the best parts about the series! There also several collectibles: the afforementioned Mui Mui, and blue flies. Flies act like Mario-esque coins in this game, they're everywhere and impossible to avoid. The blue flies and Mui Mui in extension are well hidden from the rest of the level. This is another fun feature, but unfortunately a lot of the blue fly placements are quite lazy, to put it lightly. A lot of them are hidden in invisible walls, or make you require a certain amount of LocoRoco. There's also almost no incentive to get them, either. Not a dealbraker, but still disappointing. The level design is great, and the several LocoRoco you discover are... kinda adorable. Ok, only some of them are. A really bad issue though is the repetition. The game makes no effort to keep things fresh, and it hinders the experience quite a bit. No new features are ever introduced or expanded on besides new LocoRoco colours, which are cute, but don't add much. You need to take spaced out breaks in-between worlds if you want to even think of enjoying the game. Unforunate, but not too prevalent.


Minigames and Loco House

It hurts to say this, but... both of these are really boring. Loco House is only entertaining for a few minutes and the blue flies found there are infuriating to get. And the minigames are lackluster. They stop being fun after 5 minutes. After the first completion, LocoRoco doesn't have much to offer.


Overall charm

LocoRoco is an incredibly unique game. Everything from the artstyle, to the music, to the gameplay, there is nothing that even remotely matches the style of the quality LocoRoco has to offer. Its uniqueness defined it as an absolute PSP classic, and it served as its whole identity. Game director Tsutomu Kouno comments on this even further:

"With LocoRoco, I wanted every aspect of the game, from the music to the first visual impression you get, to be unique. Polygons, complex character models, realistic shadows and lighting have become the standard for games today. I wanted to go the other way, and use a simple, effective 2D approach that would make even non-gamers say, "Hey, that's cute, I'd play that." "

—Tsutomu Kouno, on The Thoughtful Design of LocoRoco: Tsutomu Kouno Speaks

Music

You'll never gonna believe when I say this, but... the music is really unique. The game made up a whole new gibberish langauge so it could identify easily with international audiences. That language is... adorable. Just hear it for yourself!


Title Screen (Yellow) | www.zophar.net

Verdict

LocoRoco is a must-play for all PSP owners. It is incredibly unique in all aspects and is a well-made game that still holds up all these years later. For that, I give this game a solid 8/10.
